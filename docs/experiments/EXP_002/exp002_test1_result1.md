
# ADULT PROTOCOL: AIRWAY

## Overall Procedure
1. UNIVERSAL PATIENT CARE PROTOCOL
2. Assess ABC’s Respiratory Rate, Effort, and Adequacy
3. Determine if airway is adequate or inadequate
    1. For adequate airway:
        * Consider supplemental OXYGEN
            * Use caution with COPD Patients
        * Treat per specific protocol
    2. For inadequate airway:
        * BASIC MANUVERS FIRST
            * Open airway
            * Nasal / Oral Airway
            * Bag-Valve-Mask
        * Continue based on findings:
            * Not Protecting / Anticipated Loss
            * Obstructed Airway

### Not Protecting / Anticipated Loss Pathway
Criteria: Patient is not protecting airway or anticipated loss of airway.
1. CAPNOGRAPHY PROCEDURE Required
2. Consider Sedation Prior To Advanced Airway Placement IF PATIENT RESPONDS TO PAIN
    1. KETAMINE 1 mg / kg IV / IO - Usual Dose 100 mg, q 2 min prn – Max 200 mg
    2. OR MIDAZOLAM 2.5 mg IV / IO or 5 mg IM / IN – q 5 min prn – Max 10 mg
    3. OR LORazepam 1 – 2 mg IV / IO / IM / IN – q 10 min prn – Max 4 mg
        * If Midazolam or LORazepam Unavailable, See Medication Section for DiazePAM
    4. AND fentaNYL 25 mcg doses until pain tolerance prn q2 mins - Max 200 mcg
3. Consider RSI Protocol APPROVED DEPARTMENTS ONLY
    * Apply nasal cannula 15 LPM during intubation attempts
4. INTUBATION PROCEDURE
    1. Max 2 Intubation Attempts
    2. AEMT Apneic or Pulseless and Apneic Patient Only
    3. Document Failed Attempt(s)
5. Extraglottic (BIAD) AIRWAY Device
    1. No Medications Down Extraglottic (BIAD) Airway
    2. EMT Use in Pulseless & Apneic Patient Only
    3. Esophageal Disease: contraindication
6. After Advanced Airway Placement – Consider Additional Sedation
    1. KETAMINE 1 mg / kg IV / IO
    2. OR MIDAZOLAM 2.5 mg IV / IO or 5 mg IM / IN – q 10 min prn – Max 10 mg
    3. OR LORazepam 1 – 2 mg IV / IO / IM / IN – q 10 min prn – Max 4 mg
        * If Midazolam or LORazepam Unavailable, See Medication Section for DiazePAM
7. TRANSPORT to appropriate facility
8. CONTACT receiving facility
9. CONSULT Medical Control where indicated
10. APPROPRIATE transfer of care

### Obstructed Airway Pathway
Criteria: Evidence of obstructed airway on assessment.
1. SEE FOREIGN BODY AIRWAY OBSTRUCTION PROTOCOL
2. Foreign body airway obstruction (FBAO) Maneuvers
3. Direct Laryngoscopy
    * Attempt Removal with Magill Forceps
4. If unable to relieve obstruction:
    * CRICOTHYROTOMY (NEEDLE, KIT, or SURGICAL)
        * As provided and trained
        * Must Have Prior Medical Control Training / Approval
        * CAPNOGRAPHY PROCEDURE Required

## Indications
* Apnea
* Coughing
* Choking
* Inability to speak
* Unresponsive
* Burns
* Trauma

## Signs and Symptoms
* Witnessed aspiration
* Sudden episode of choking
* Gagging
* Audible stridor
* Change in skin color
* Decreased LOC
* Increased or decreased Respiratory rate
* Labored breathing
* Unproductive cough

## Differential Diagnosis
* Cardiac arrest
* Respiratory arrest
* Anaphylaxis
* Esophageal obstruction

## Key Points
* EMT may only place extraglottic (BIAD) airways in pulseless and apneic patients.
* Advanced EMT’s may perform orotracheal intubation or place an extraglottic (BIAD) airway on apneic or apneic and pulseless patients.
* Capnography is mandatory with all methods of intubation. Document results.
* Maintain C-spine immobilization for patients with suspected spinal injury.
* Do not assume hyperventilation is psychogenic - use oxygen, not a paper bag.
* Sellick's maneuver should be used to assist with difficult intubations.
* Paramedics should consider using an extraglottic (BIAD) airway if they are unable to Intubate. Consider c-collar to maintain ETT placement for all intubated patients to maintain tube.
* Consider the use of intubation aids such as a bougie or video laryngoscope to facilitate intubation.
* Ketamine use in pregnancy is a risk / benefit assessment per case. Consult Medical Control if there are questions.
* Ketamine is the preferred induction agent with asthmatic patients

## Other Information
* Extraglottic airway device / BIAD (Blind Insertion Airway Device) 
    * Examples (not limited to); iGel, King Airway, LMA, Etc.
* Differentiate airway obstruction from esophageal obstruction.
* If esophageal obstruction, place patient in position of comfort, suction as needed and treat pain.
