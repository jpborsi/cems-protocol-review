
# AIRWAY

## Overall Procedure
1. UNIVERSAL PATIENT CARE PROTOCOL
2. Assess ABC’s Respiratory Rate, Effort, and Adequacy
3. Continue with further steps as necessary
    1. Consider Supplemental OXYGEN  
        - COPD Patients
    2. Treat per specific protocol

    - If airway is adequate:  
        - Monitor as appropriate.

    - If airway is inadequate:  
        - BASIC MANUVERS FIRST
            * Open airway
            * Nasal / Oral Airway
            * Bag-Valve-Mask

    - If patient is Not Protecting / Anticipated Loss of airway:  
        - CAPNOGRAPHY PROCEDURE Required
        - Consider Sedation Prior To Advanced Airway Placement IF PATIENT RESPONDS TO PAIN
            * KETAMINE 1 mg/kg IV/IO – Usual Dose 100 mg, q 2 min prn – Max 200 mg
            * OR MIDAZOLAM 2.5 mg IV/IO or 5 mg IM/IN – q 5 min prn – Max 10 mg
            * OR LORazepam 1–2 mg IV/IO/IM/IN – q 10 min prn – Max 4 mg
            * If Midazolam or LORazepam Unavailable, See Medication Section for DiazePAM
            * AND fentaNYL 25 mcg doses until pain tolerance prn q2 mins - Max 200 mcg
        - Consider RSI Protocol APPROVED DEPARTMENTS ONLY
        - Apply nasal cannula 15 LPM during intubation attempts
        - INTUBATION PROCEDURE
            * Max 2 Intubation Attempts
            * AEMT Apneic or Pulseless and Apneic Patient Only
            * Document Failed Attempt(s)
        - Extraglottic (BIAD) AIRWAY Device
            * No Medications Down Extraglottic (BIAD) Airway
            * EMT Use in Pulseless & Apneic Patient Only
            * Esophageal Disease

        - Additional Sedation Required After Advanced Airway Placement:
            * KETAMINE 1 mg/kg IV/IO – Usual Dose 100 mg, q 2 min prn – Max 200 mg
            * OR MIDAZOLAM 2.5 mg IV/IO or 5 mg IM/IN – q 10 min prn – Max 10 mg
            * OR LORazepam 1–2 mg IV/IO/IM/IN – q 10 min prn – Max 4 mg
            * If Midazolam or LORazepam Unavailable, See Medication Section for DiazePAM

    - If Obstructed Airway:
        - SEE FOREIGN BODY AIRWAY OBSTRUCTION PROTOCOL
        - Foreign body airway obstruction (FBAO) Maneuvers
        - Direct Laryngoscopy
            * Attempt Removal with Magill Forceps
        - CRICOTHYROTOMY (NEEDLE, KIT, or SURGICAL as provided and trained)
            * Must Have Prior Medical Control Training / Approval
            * CAPNOGRAPHY PROCEDURE Required

4. TRANSPORT to appropriate facility
5. CONTACT receiving facility
6. CONSULT Medical Control where indicated
7. APPROPRIATE transfer of care

### Not Protecting / Anticipated Loss vs. Obstructed Airway
**Criteria for path selection:**
- Take the “Not Protecting / Anticipated Loss” path if the airway is not protected or anticipated to be lost.
- Take the “Obstructed Airway” path if there is a visible/obvious obstruction.

#### Not Protecting / Anticipated Loss
1. CAPNOGRAPHY PROCEDURE Required
2. Sedation as needed if patient responds to pain
3. Intubation procedure with a maximum of 2 attempts (AEMT Apneic or Pulseless and Apneic Patient Only), document failed attempts.
4. Attempt Extraglottic (BIAD) Airway Device if needed
5. Apply additional sedation after advanced airway placement if required

#### Obstructed Airway
1. SEE FOREIGN BODY AIRWAY OBSTRUCTION PROTOCOL
2. Perform FBAO maneuvers
3. Direct Laryngoscopy - Attempt Removal with Magill Forceps
4. If necessary, perform Cricothyrotomy (Needle, Kit, or Surgical, as provided and trained; must have prior medical control training/approval)
5. CAPNOGRAPHY PROCEDURE Required

## Indications
* Apnea
* Coughing
* Choking
* Inability to speak
* Unresponsive
* Burns
* Trauma

## Signs and Symptoms
* Witnessed aspiration
* Sudden episode of choking
* Gagging
* Audible stridor
* Change in skin color
* Decreased LOC
* Increased or decreased respiratory rate
* Labored breathing
* Unproductive cough

## Differential Diagnosis
* Cardiac arrest
* Respiratory arrest
* Anaphylaxis
* Esophageal obstruction

## Key Points
* EMT may only place extraglottic (BIAD) airways in pulseless and apneic patients.
* Advanced EMT’s may perform orotracheal intubation or place an extraglottic (BIAD) airway on apneic or apneic and pulseless patients.
* Capnography is mandatory with all methods of intubation. Document results.
* Maintain C-spine immobilization for patients with suspected spinal injury.
* Do not assume hyperventilation is psychogenic - use oxygen, not a paper bag.
* Sellick's maneuver should be used to assist with difficult intubations.
* Paramedics should consider using an extraglottic (BIAD) airway if they are unable to intubate. Consider c-collar to maintain ETT placement for all intubated patients to maintain tube.
* Consider the use of intubation aids such as a bougie or video laryngoscope to facilitate intubation.
* Ketamine use in pregnancy is a risk / benefit assessment per case. Consult Medical Control if there are questions.
* Ketamine is the preferred induction agent with asthmatic patients.

## Other Information
* Extraglottic airway device / BIAD (Blind Insertion Airway Device) Examples (not limited to): iGel, King Airway, LMA, Etc.
* Differentiate airway obstruction from esophageal obstruction. If esophageal obstruction, place patient in position of comfort, suction as needed and treat pain.
